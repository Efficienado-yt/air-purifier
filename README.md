# airbox-model

A cost, airflow, noise and filter-life model for DIY PC-fan air purifier boxes, compared on one metric against store-bought purifiers and commercial kits: **five-year dollars per average delivered cfm**.

Companion repository for the Efficienado video on building a quiet, cheap air purifier from PC fans and furnace filters.  Every number quoted in the video comes out of this code, and every input the code uses is in `params.json` with the source and the date it was read.

## Headline result

In average indoor air, under a 35 dB noise cap at 3 ft, the cheapest clean air over five years is eight Arctic P14 Pro PST fans at 50% speed on two 20x25x2 MERV 11 filters: about 285 cfm of clean air when new, 33 dB, about $93 to build, about $1.29 per average cfm over five years with one filter swap at 30 months.  The four-filter cube on the same fans and media delivers 303 cfm for $120 and $1.32 per cfm with no swap inside five years.  The best appliance in the comparison, the AirFanta 3Pro, comes to about $2.26 per cfm; a refurbished Winix C545 on generic filters about $2.54.

The full tables are in `airbox_report.md` (markdown) and `airbox_results.xlsx` (every build, every scenario, every input).

## What it does

For every combination of fan, fan count, filter, filter count and speed step (about 106,000 builds), the model:

1. finds the **operating airflow** where the fan curve crosses the filter's resistance curve;
2. converts airflow to **CADR** (clean air delivery rate) with a build factor for leakage and the media's single-pass catch when new;
3. estimates **noise at 3 ft** from the fan's bench dBA, the fan count and a bench-to-room offset;
4. models **electret media decay** over time and picks the **filter swap interval** that minimises dollars per delivered cfm;
5. totals **five-year cost**: fans, controller, tape, the first filter set, later filter sets at real pack prices, and 24/7 electricity;
6. ranks everything by **five-year dollars per average delivered cfm**, under noise caps of 30, 35, 40, 45 and 50 dB, in three indoor-air scenarios (average, poor, wildfire-smoke month).

Store-bought purifiers and commercial kits go through the same accounting, so every row lands on the same metric.

## Quick start

Requires Python 3.9 or later.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 run.py
```

`run.py` writes `airbox_results.xlsx` and `airbox_report.md` next to itself and prints the report.  A full run takes about a minute.

To change an input, edit `params.json` (prices, electricity rate, filters, appliances, scenarios, rooms) or `fans_data.py` (fan curves) and rerun.

## How the numbers are built

**Airflow.**  Each fan's Cybenetics curve gives free-air cfm and maximum static pressure at each speed step.  The model draws a straight line between them and finds where it crosses the filter's resistance curve (the FilterBoxBuilder MERV 13 polynomial, with multipliers for other media grades and an effective-area factor for deeper pleats).  The straight-line fan curve is conservative by roughly 8 to 10% on airflow.

**CADR.**  Airflow x build factor (0.85 for a taped cardboard build, 0.90 for a sealed kit) x the media's single-pass smoke-class catch when new (0.85 for MERV 11 and MERV 13 at box velocity, fitted to seven measured boxes; see Validation).

**Noise.**  Per-fan Cybenetics chamber dBA + 10 log(fans) + a 2 dB bench-to-3 ft offset anchored on one HouseFresh measurement of the Nukit Tempest.  Appliances use HouseFresh's measured readings at 3 ft.  Steps at the chamber floor (6 dBA) are excluded.

**Filter life.**  Electret media loses catch as its charge decays: catch(t) = floor + (new - floor) x exp(-t / tau).  tau scales with the box's media area per cfm and with the scenario's particle load.  The swap interval is optimised, not fixed; "no swap inside five years" is a legitimate answer and the report also shows each headline build with a swap forced inside 24 and 36 months.

**Cost.**  Fans and filters are priced by the packs a builder would actually buy (the cheapest combination of packs that covers the build, spares paid for), plus the controller, tape, splitters where a non-daisy-chaining fan needs them, and five years of 24/7 electricity at the marginal residential rate in `params.json`.  Appliances include their first filter set and later sets at their stated cadence.

**Rooms.**  Units of one option are stacked until the room reaches 5 air changes an hour; noise adds 10 log(units).

## The Efficienado Reference Box

A real box built before this model existed: three Noctua NF-P12 redux-1700 fans plus one NF-A14 FLX on four 16x25x1 filters, driven by a 3 to 12 V voltage dial.  Its noise was measured at 3 ft with a recorder anchored to HouseFresh's published readings of a Winix C545 in the same position (44.6 dB at full dial, 33.1 dB at half dial, 24.0 dB room floor).  Its airflow is from the fan datasheets by fan law, not measured.  It appears in every table as a yellow row, priced both as bought and at current fan prices, and is excluded from the "best DIY" selection because its as-bought price includes a spare fan that was already on hand.

## Assumptions and limitations

The model is a comparison tool, not a lab.  The things most worth knowing before quoting a number from it:

- **Media catch is fitted, not measured.**  The 0.85 single-pass catch for MERV 13 (range 0.78 to 0.90) is the build x catch product pinned by seven measured boxes; the two halves are not separately identifiable.  MERV 11 at 0.85 (per-box 0.80 to 0.88) decides the winning media, and at 0.80 MERV 13 wins every cap, so MERV 11 vs MERV 13 is a tie inside the model's error.  MERV 16 catch and resistance rest on manufacturer claims.
- **Filter life is the least-sourced piece.**  The decay time constant sits between two anchors that give 3 to 16 months once put on the same duty cycle; the 0.42 relative floor comes from duct-velocity data with no measurement at box velocity, and the "no swap in five years" result flips below a floor of 0.376.  The 2 inch vs 1 inch margin is inside 1%.  Rising resistance from dust loading is not modeled for DIY boxes.
- **Pressure drop and airflow.**  The MERV 13 resistance polynomial is uncertain by about 2x in absolute terms at box velocity; the seven-box validation absorbs the level.  The pleat-depth factor is fitted to three FilterBoxBuilder readings and 4 inch = 2 inch is an assumption.
- **Noise.**  The 2 dB bench-to-room offset rests on a single anchor.  Spec-sheet fans (EST rows) are on a different scale from the Cybenetics bench.  The Reference Box's airflow is from datasheets and only two dial positions were measured.
- **Appliance rows mix methods.**  Quiet-speed CADR for some units is a clear-time proxy applied to the AHAM rating; the 0.95 loading derate is a first estimate from one unit in one lab; watts are held constant over filter life; every price is a read on a stated date at the pack sizes a builder would buy.

Each of these is written up in full, with the source and the flip point where one is known, in the `_` notes in `params.json`.

## Validation

The model was run against seven measured PC-fan boxes (Nukit Tempest, CleanAirKits Luggable XL-7, XL Ultra and two 5-fan Luggables, the Tex-Air six-fan cube, AirFanta 3Pro) using HouseFresh PM1, AHAM-verified smoke, CleanAirKits chamber and Tex-Air measurements.  The current model predicts 0.89 to 1.05 of measured CADR, mean about 0.97.  Airflow tracks within about plus or minus 6%.  Details in `params.json` under `filter_types._validation_2026-09-07` and in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Modifying the model

- **Add a fan:** append an entry to `FANS` in `fans_data.py` with its Cybenetics per-step curve, and a pack price list to `fan_packs` in `params.json` under the same name.
- **Add a filter:** add a row to `filters` (`[width, height, depth, legacy single price, media type, source]`) and a pack list to `filter_packs`.
- **Add an appliance:** add a row to `appliances` (`[price, quiet watts, quiet CADR, filters $/yr, quiet dB at 3 ft, note]`); use `appliance_options` if its filter cadence is not yearly or it ships with extra sets.
- **Change a scenario, room, noise cap or the electricity rate:** edit the matching key in `params.json`.

Rerun `run.py`.  The Params sheet in the workbook records exactly what the run used.

## Measuring noise

`sound_level.py` prints the A-weighted level timeline of a WAV file in dBFS.  Record the box and a reference source with a published dB figure at the same distance and gain, then anchor the dBFS readings to the published level.  It needs numpy and scipy (both in `requirements.txt`).

```bash
python3 sound_level.py recording.wav
```
